# Voice and copy

How the site sounds, and the rules for every word on it.

## Voice

Calm, clear and specific, like a skilled person explaining something they made with care.

- **Plain over clever.** If a headline needs explaining, rewrite it.
- **Specific over grand.** "Bills due in 3 days, right on your home screen" beats "Take control of your finances".
- **Short.** Short sentences, short paragraphs, one idea per section.
- **Confident, not loud.** No exclamation marks, no hype. Let the facts carry the weight.
- **Honest.** Say what exists today. Say "in development" for what doesn't.

## Language

- **US English** spelling: color, organize, center. It matches the Caps app and caps.noozytech.com ([decision 0004](../decisions/0004-english-only-us-spelling.md)).
- **Sentence case** everywhere: headings, buttons, navigation and page titles. Product names keep their capitals.
- **Names:** NoOzyTech, Caps Money Manager (first mention on a page), Caps, Caps Pro, Fire & Safety ERP.

## Headlines

- Two lines at most on a laptop screen, which usually means 8 words or fewer.
- One idea. Name the benefit or the thing itself, not an adjective about it.
- No question headlines, and no puns that hide the meaning.
- If a headline could sit unchanged on a competitor's site, rewrite it.

## Supporting text

- 20 words or fewer under a headline.
- No line wider than about 75 characters. The design handles this; don't work around it.

## Buttons and links

A verb plus what people get. The same action always has the same label.

| Action | Label |
|---|---|
| Install Caps | Get it on Google Play (a text button, without Google's logo) |
| Go to the Caps website | Explore Caps |
| ERP early access | Request early access |
| Email the company | Email us, or show the address itself |

- Never "Click here", or "Learn more" and "Read more" on their own. Link text must make sense out of context, because screen readers can list links without their surroundings.
- Don't put "→" on every link. Use an arrow only where it means "this takes you somewhere else".

## Proof

- Use only facts from `docs/product/`. Never invent numbers, quotes, logos or reviews.
- When proof is missing, write `[NEED: …]` and ask. Never publish a page that contains `[NEED`.
- Write numbers as numerals: "160 currencies", "6 languages", "5 devices".

## Words and patterns to avoid

They make copy sound generic or machine-written.

- **Words:** elevate, seamless, unleash, unlock, empower, supercharge, revolutionize, transform, next-gen, cutting-edge, game-changer, world-class, best-in-class, robust, leverage, harness, delve, effortless, journey, and "in today's fast-paced world". "Solution" only in the approved phrase "enterprise solution".
- **Patterns:**
  - "It's not X, it's Y."
  - "The result?" and other question-then-answer reveals.
  - Three adjectives in a row ("simple, powerful, beautiful").
  - Lists of things a product doesn't do, unless each item is a real, checkable promise. "No ads, no data selling, and no bank logins" is fine: all three are true of Caps.
  - Em dashes as a habit. Use a period or a comma.
  - Emoji in headings or body copy.

## Punctuation

- Curly quotes and apostrophes (“ ” ’) and a real ellipsis (…).
- En dash for ranges: 9–5, 2026–2027.
- A non-breaking space between a number and its unit: 10&nbsp;MB.
- A multiplication sign in sizes: 1080×1920.
- No period at the end of headlines or button labels.

## Accessibility in copy

- Alt text says what in the image matters. A screenshot: "Caps home screen showing one total balance across three wallets". A decorative image: empty alt text.
- Don't point at things by position or color alone, like "the button on the right" or "the green link".

## SEO in copy

- Write for people first. Use the words people actually search with where they read naturally: money manager, expense tracker, budget app, ERP for fire & safety companies.
- One main topic per page. The page title, the H1 and the first paragraph agree on it.
- Never repeat keywords to rank. Search engines treat keyword stuffing as spam.
